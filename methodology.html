Berkeley City Council Scorecard — Methodology

Version: 1.0 · 11 August 2026
Status: Normative. This document governs. Where the implementation disagrees with it,
the implementation is wrong and the discrepancy is technical debt, recorded in
SCORING.md.

The previous methodology is preserved as METHODOLOGY_LEGACY.md. It described a scoring
system this document supersedes.

1. The question

Does the member exercise municipal governing authority competently, deliberately,
accountably, and with appropriate prioritization?

That is the whole standard. Everything below serves it.

It is deliberately not "does the member align with taxpayer interests." That earlier
framing pulled consistently toward less spending is better, which is a policy
preference wearing the costume of a standard. A member can enthusiastically fund a
project and govern it excellently — establish the problem, compare interventions,
specify outcomes, determine lifecycle cost, secure operating funding, pause at an unsafe
gate, deliver it. That is good governance, and a methodology that cannot say so is
measuring the wrong thing.

A low score is not evidence the model is too harsh. A high score is not a design
objective. There is no target grade distribution, and no threshold may be adjusted to
produce one.

2. Scope is not quality

The Category framework answers whether and how the City should be involved. It does not
answer whether a decision was well made.

  • Category 1 — Berkeley must ensure it is done. Core municipal outcomes, and the
    routine administration that keeps them running.
  • Category 2 — Berkeley should act with partners. Legitimate public purposes where
    the City should normally enable, coordinate, regulate or contract rather than become
    the operator.
  • Category 3 — Berkeley should not own or continue it. Work outside the City's proper
    scale, duplicating a capable provider, lacking essential partners, or materially
    diluting Category 1 performance. Not a moral judgment about the subject.

A Category 1 classification establishes only that the City has a legitimate
responsibility to act. It creates neither a presumption of good governance nor a penalty
for acting.

It does not mean taking the work on was wise — much of what a city "must" do is mandatory
only because an earlier council decided it would be. It does not mean the work was handled
well. It does not mean money inside it was well spent.

This matters because every governance failure this site documents concerns Category 1
work. Street safety, public safety budgeting, homeless-services siting. If Category 1
implied "fine," the site's most serious findings would be immunized by their own
classification.

Two questions, always separate:

  1. Role — is the City acting in the right category, at the right level?
  2. Quality — given that role, did the member help make a disciplined, evidence-based,
    fiscally responsible decision?

2.1 Two review tests

A project can fail twice, and the failures are scored separately.

Premise review. Was the original problem sufficiently established, and was the chosen
intervention justified against alternatives and measurable outcomes? What problem was
documented? How was its scale established? Why this intervention? What alternatives were
compared? What outcomes was it expected to improve? What evidence would later show
whether it worked?

Continuation review. Given current evidence, costs, performance and fiscal
conditions, does the inherited course still deserve to advance?

Collapsing them produces both false exoneration and false blame. "The project was
already approved" answers only the historical authorization question — it establishes
neither that the premise was sound nor that continuation is justified now.

3. What is scored

3.1 Acts, not vocabulary

No score may change because a member used a word or discussed a subject. Not for
foreign policy, policing, taxation, surveillance, homelessness, climate, immigration, or
any other topic.

Keyword lists may find candidates for human review. They may never assign or alter a
grade. Adding or deleting a word in a transcript must not move a score.

This is not a preference. It is the finding of four failed attempts, recorded in
METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md. The most recent produced a classifier where 43% of what it
flagged as out-of-scope was core municipal business — police oversight, surveillance
procurement, and tax policy, which is the site's own central subject.

3.2 Style is not evidence; substance is

Linguistic style is not scored. No warmth, humility, first-person usage, verbosity,
collegiality-by-tone, or similarity to colleagues. Personality is not governance.

A member's words are evidence when they contain a factual claim, commitment, governing
principle, explanation, or other substantive representation relevant to official
conduct.

3.3 No policy-area variable

No service area may be a scoring input. Not homelessness, policing, transportation,
climate, or economic development.

A service area may accumulate many low ratings if its decisions repeatedly fail the
general rubric — that conclusion must emerge from the decisions, not be embedded in the
formula. The retired homeless-services alignment variable made one policy area roughly a
quarter of every grade through a coefficient. Its underlying conduct is now scored under
the ordinary rules: funding without outcome evidence under Oversight, defending
continuation without examining alternatives under Decision Quality, seeking new resources
without examining existing spending under Fiscal Stewardship.

That is a harder criticism, not a softer one. "You scored badly because you support
homeless services" is dismissible. "Here are the ordinary rules of competent governance,
and here are the decisions that failed them" is not.

3.4 The material decision set

Materiality first. The scorecard distinguishes governance performance; it does not
adjudicate every vote. Approving the minutes must not dilute approving a facility with no
operating plan.

A decision is material when it commits substantial money or creates a recurring
obligation; creates, expands, continues, restructures or terminates a program; acquires
or disposes of a significant asset; sets durable policy; directs substantial staff work;
consumes attention disproportionate to the decision required; responds or fails to
respond to an audit or missed target; is a Category 3 action consuming any municipal
resource; or presents a principle–action contradiction.

Routine administration is recorded, not scored.

Dollar value is the wrong trigger. Tested against the corpus, a $1M threshold selects
100 items, of which 70 are Category 1 vendor procurement — landscape maintenance, culvert
repair, fire apparatus. A contract becomes material when the commitment behind it is at
issue, not because of its face value.

3.5 The wrapper rule

Classify the governing act before Council, not every element inside its administrative
wrapper. Updating a capital project list is Category 1 administration even when the list
contains a Category 3 project; the embedded project retains its own character and is
scored separately where material. Category does not propagate in either direction. The
purpose of wrapper classification is accurate taxonomy, not score laundering.

3.6 Votes are governance acts

Scope scoring may not be limited to authorship. Measured against this corpus, only 25 of
120 material decisions carry an attributable author, so for most material Council
decisions the binding vote — not authorship — is the primary observable exercise of an
individual member's governing authority. A scorecard built on authorship alone would be
blind to roughly four fifths of the record.

Conversely, opposing, narrowing, defeating or redirecting a Category 3 proposal is not
penalized and may earn credit. Polarity is mandatory.

4. Dimensions

Five, each answering a different question. Weights are reserved to calibration.

Dimension Question
Role and Scope Is the City acting within its proper role, in the proper form?
Fiscal and Lifecycle Stewardship Are money, assets, staff capacity and future obligations treated as scarce?
Decision Quality Did the member help find the best available solution rather than approve the one presented?
Oversight and Results Is approval treated as the start of accountability rather than the end of the decision?
Institutional Responsibility Can the public rely on the member's stated principles and factual claims as an account of how they govern?

4.1 Rating scale

Each material decision receives 0–4 on each applicable dimension.

Rating Meaning
4 — Exemplary Materially improves the decision: sharpens scope, exposes tradeoffs, tests assumptions, compares credible alternatives, secures lifecycle or outcome analysis, corrects a failing course.
3 — Sound Meets the expected standard of competent municipal governance.
2 — Mixed Defensible but materially incomplete.
1 — Deficient Advances or accepts a materially weak decision despite identifiable deficiencies, or acts against a clearly stated principle without credible explanation.
0 — Serious failure Causes, advances, protects, or fails to confront a major governance failure.

3 is the expected standard, not praise. Holding office, attending, voting on routine
business and being collegial do not exceed it.

4.2 When a dimension applies

A dimension applies when the record contains evidence bearing on the question that
dimension asks — not because the decision could in principle raise it.

The default is not applicable. Marking a dimension applicable and rating it 3 because
nothing looks wrong is prohibited: it converts absence of evidence into evidence of
competence.

Where a dimension is plainly engaged but the record does not show what the member did,
mark it inapplicable and note it. Accumulated notes are a finding about the corpus,
not the member.

Authorship alone does not establish Decision Quality. Authoring places a matter before
Council — a Role and Scope fact. Decision Quality requires a decision-shaping act: an
amendment or substitute, a referral for analysis, a documented question that tested an
assumption, a stated comparison of alternatives, an attached condition or exit criterion.
Authorship, co-sponsorship and a recorded vote are not, without more, decision-shaping
acts.

Institutional Responsibility requires a representation to test — a stated principle,
criterion, factual claim, commitment or explanation, and an act to compare it against.
It does not apply to procedural regularity, to the form an act took, or to ordinary
participation.

4.3 Principle–action inconsistency

Officials may change their minds, and may conclude a general principle does not control a
particular case. What is scoreable is unreconciled inconsistency in the exercise and
explanation of public power — not hypocrisy, and not motive.

All four conditions must hold: a clear stated principle; a comparable later matter; a
material official act inconsistent with it; and no adequate reconciliation.

Describe the contradiction; do not diagnose motive. Do not use "dishonest," "bad faith,"
or comparable labels absent independent evidence of intent. A member who identifies the
tension and explains why the principle yields differently has governed well.

4.4 Silence

Scoreable only when the member had an identifiable responsibility or opportunity, the
problem was material, the evidence was reasonably available, and the record shows no
meaningful response before or during a consequential decision.

No generic newsletter-silence, speech-silence, or low-word-count penalty.

4.5 Sparse evidence

Thin records are reported as thin. No default or invented score may be assigned
because evidence is missing. Where coverage cannot support a responsible composite,
publish the records and withhold the grade. Transcript word count is never an eligibility
rule.

4.6 Absolute, not relative

No member's score may depend on another's. No normalization across the council. Adding or
removing an officeholder cannot move anyone's grade.

5. The duties of office

5.1 The inventory duty

Someone stepping into the role must take inventory of the initiatives already in
motion. They were binned Category 1 or 2 by a previous Council. The incoming member owes
the office a judgment about whether they are in the right bin, and whether they are moving
toward meaningful objectives with metrics.

This is not a reform proposal. It is what taking responsibility for a portfolio means
wherever one custodian succeeds another. Administering an inherited portfolio without
assessing it is not neutrality — it is a decision to continue everything, made by not
deciding.

The questions: Is it in the right bin? If Category 1, is this the right discharge of the
responsibility? If Category 2, has involvement drifted into operation? Is there a stated
objective? Are there metrics anyone reads? What recurring obligation does it create, and
has that been funded or assumed? What gate comes next, and how reversible is the position
after it?

On accession, and periodically thereafter. A portfolio assessed once in a four-year term
is not assessed.

This is reasonable triage, not exhaustive historical review. Most items return healthy
continuation.

5.2 The continuation question

Before voting to continue a material commitment, a member owes the office an answer to
one question: is this thing, which the City is legitimately responsible for, in a state
that warrants continuing it?

Category 1 does not answer that question. Treating the category as the answer is how
predecessor decisions become permanent apparent necessities.

The duty is discharged by asking. The finding is never that the wrong conclusion was
reached; it is that no judgment was formed. A member who, asked afterward why they voted
for a commitment, must go and find out what it costs to run, has demonstrated the question
was not asked before the vote.

5.3 Authority creates responsibility

When an officeholder has sufficient notice of a material governance deficiency and
possesses practical authority to force its reconsideration, exercising reasonable judgment
about whether and how to use that authority is part of the duty of office.

"Opportunity" alone reads as optional — he could have pulled it from consent. The
standard is stronger: under the circumstances, responsible performance of the office
called for doing something. Not a duty to reach any particular conclusion — a duty to
force the unresolved question into the decision process while meaningful choices remain.

Success is not the test. Using the office is. A member need not have the votes to
reverse a trajectory in order to bear responsibility for responding to it.

5.4 Consent is a governance choice

Consent is an affirmative judgment that an item has earned routine continuation, not the
absence of a judgment about it.

Consent should mean: this is sufficiently healthy, routine, understood and consistent with
existing policy that Council need not spend scarce deliberative capacity on it. It must
not mean: staff placed it on consent, therefore continuation is presumed.

The triage test is cheap. What is this? When and why was it authorized? Has anything
material changed? Is it on budget? Are promised outcomes or prerequisites present? Are
there unresolved audits? Does it deepen a recurring obligation? Is an irreversible gate
approaching?

Most items return healthy continuation and stay on consent. That is the point —
competent triage preserves attention for the few matters that need governing judgment.

Where the check surfaces an unresolved governing question, the item does not belong on
consent. Pulling it does not imply opposition; it implies the question cannot
responsibly be buried inside routine continuation.

Measured against this corpus (re-derived September 2026 from the agendas, not the linked
votes — see METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md §4.3): 759 consent items, 118 at or above
$1M totaling $5.51B, across 37 of the 41 meetings carrying consent — 3.2 items per
affected meeting. The objection that portfolio review would produce nine-hour meetings
is not supported by the volume.

5.5 Heightened district responsibility

A member has heightened portfolio responsibility for material City projects, assets,
services and conditions concentrated within their district.

This heightens notice and opportunity, not culpability. District representation makes
the constructive-notice question easier to answer for that member — it does not answer it.

It cuts both ways. The duty is to know, examine, surface and govern — not to oppose,
protect, or deliver whatever constituents want. A district member is not a parochial
veto-holder, and a member who used district standing to shield a deficient project from
scrutiny would be exercising the same responsibility badly.

Support for an objective does not diminish the duty to ensure successful delivery. A
member may appropriately Stop or Change a favored project precisely because unresolved
deficiencies threaten the objective they support.

Applied identically to a failing fire station, a marina capital project, a street
reconstruction, or a cultural facility. If the rule only ever fires on projects the
scorecard dislikes, it is being applied wrongly.

5.6 Start / Stop / Continue / Change

Council should routinely subject material activity to four questions: what should begin,
what should terminate or be refused advancement, what has earned continued commitment, and
what should continue only after redesign or rescoping.

Stop includes projects not yet ready to proceed. A project need not have failed
operationally before responsible governance requires a halt.

Continuation is itself a decision. It should be justified rather than assumed.

6. Inherited governance

Prior Council approval establishes history, not necessity.

Taking office does not create responsibility for the origin of inherited conditions. It
does create responsibility for governing them from that point forward.

Succession changes responsibility for origin; it does not interrupt responsibility for
response.

A member assumes responsibility for an inherited trajectory when, after sufficient notice
and opportunity, they affirmatively continue, extend, protect, or materially fail to
challenge it.

6.1 Decision gates and manufactured inevitability

Council must not use progressive commitment to substitute for unresolved
justification. Advancing a project in ways that create sunk costs, contractual
obligations, physical facts on the ground, or political expectations reduces the City's
practical ability to reconsider it later. Those effects are themselves material
consequences of the decision to advance.

Where an unresolved deficiency becomes harder to address after the next decision gate,
the duty to scrutinize increases before that gate is crossed. Scrutiny is owed most at
the point where it can still change something.

A badly governed project can become effectively irreversible before completion. Where
that happens, the failure is not only the final commitment — it is the sequence that
removed future choices.

Do not assert motive. Intent is rarely provable and is not needed. State the
institutional effect: continuing construction increased the cost and political difficulty
of reconsideration while the operating model remained unresolved.

Incremental commitment is ordinary and often correct. The failure is incremental
commitment used instead of resolving a known prerequisite. The test is whether the
question was being worked while the project advanced, or merely deferred.

6.2 Notice: actual and constructive

Actual notice exists when the record establishes that the member or office received,
discussed, acknowledged, voted on, or demonstrably encountered the information.

Constructive notice may be found when an issue was sufficiently prominent, formally
documented, relevant to a live responsibility, and reasonably discoverable through ordinary
portfolio review. Relevant factors: materiality; prominence and formality of the source;
whether Council formally received it; whether it remained unresolved; whether related
decisions kept coming before Council.

Constructive notice is a standard of reasonable governing diligence, not a device for
imposing hindsight liability. It must not rest on the mere existence of information
somewhere in City records.

Actual and constructive notice must never be silently equated. A finding resting on
constructive notice is weaker and must say so.

6.3 Temporal responsibility

Time affects responsibility according to the nature of the governing act. Decay applies
to isolated past acts, not to unresolved conditions under continuing jurisdiction.

The operative test: is this old conduct, or is this an old problem the current
officeholder keeps choosing not to solve?

  • Discrete act — bounded in time. Attaches to officeholders with notice and
    opportunity then. Decays. A member elected later does not inherit culpability merely
    because consequences persist.
  • Continuing trajectory — an unresolved condition repeatedly presented to current
    officeholders. Does not decay. Responsibility refreshes at each material gate and may
    accumulate through repeated non-intervention. Ten years of worsening condition is not one
    ten-year-old failure fading away; it is a sequence of renewed opportunities to govern.
  • Inherited consequence — damage caused before arrival. Responsibility attaches to the
    current response, once authority, notice and a realistic corrective opportunity
    coexist.

The rule is symmetric: years of correction accrue credit that likewise does not decay.

6.4 Opportunity accounting

A trajectory record carries three counts, never one: opportunities identified,
opportunities adjudicated, opportunities unresolved.

An opportunity count is not a finding. It records where responsibility could have
accrued, not whether it did. Left as a bare number it hardens into culpability before
adjudication — "six budget cycles" starts reading as six failures.

Reported as: six material opportunities identified; four adjudicated; two unresolved.
Where unresolved is greater than zero, any narrative must say so.

7. Attribution

The unit is member × material decision.

7.1 Three predicates

An attribution must establish all three before a role is assigned:

  1. Notice — what did the member reasonably know, and when? Must precede the act it
    indicts.
  2. Opportunity — what practical authority did they hold at a gate still open to them?
    Read with §5.3: the question is not merely whether an instrument existed but whether
    responsible performance called for its use.
  3. Response — what did they do with it, including doing nothing, stated as such.

Where no opportunity is identified, the member is not a corrective-failure case.
Absence of an instrument is not conduct.

Opportunity is typed, not free text: consent_pull, motion, amendment, deferral,
request_analysis, request_audit, public_challenge, other, none_identified.

7.2 Responsibility classes

Class Meaning
Origin Materially originated, sponsored, designed or drove the course.
Affirmative continuation Knowingly acted to continue or deepen it after deficiencies warranted reconsideration.
Corrective failure Inherited it, had notice and meaningful opportunity, materially failed to force reconsideration.
Protection from reconsideration Used official power or procedure to prevent warranted scrutiny. Categorically different from ratifying.
Corrective action Opposed, exposed, narrowed, conditioned, delayed, redirected, or attempted correction.

Mere tenure during a failure does not establish responsibility. Absence is scored in
attendance, not here. Supporting a course before the defect was reasonably knowable
establishes no responsibility for the failure — treating it as such is hindsight scoring.

7.3 Participation

Responsibility scales with relationship to the act: author and sponsor highest, binding
vote next, co-sponsorship and material amendment below, advocacy without a binding act
lower, passing comment not at all. The ladder's shape is normative; its coefficients are
reserved to calibration.

7.4 Uniform failures

Where a record genuinely differentiates roles, attribution differentiates. Where it does
not, uniform attribution is the accurate description. An attribution scheme producing
sharply different roles on a uniform failure would be inventing distinctions the record
does not contain.

A body that acts as one unit fails as one unit. Individual attribution exists to
distinguish roles within a shared failure, not to sort members.

8. Material Governance Failures

A Material Governance Failure is a decision, series of decisions, or sustained failure to
act that materially alters the City's assets, finances, services, institutions,
neighborhoods or public realm; produces substantial and durable adverse consequences; and
cannot readily be corrected through ordinary subsequent governance.

Four characteristics: transformative consequence; durability; governance causation; scale.
An MGF may arise from one dramatic decision or from cumulative governance over time.

8.1 Two lanes

The earlier definition required a material premise failure plus authoritative
notice. That describes one subtype and was mistaken for the whole category. It fits
misrepresentation cases and excludes the broader class — deteriorating infrastructure,
progressive commitment without a viable operating model, conditions allowed to destroy an
established public use.

Decision-integrity MGF. Material premise failure, misrepresentation, or authoritative
contradiction before final action. Qualification: high-stakes governmental action; material
premise failure; authoritative notice before final action; practical opportunity to cure.

Stewardship / trajectory MGF. A consequential City condition or commitment allowed to
become seriously impaired, difficult to reverse, or structurally embedded despite
authority, notice and meaningful opportunities to intervene. Qualification: high-stakes
condition or commitment; material and persisting deficiency; notice — actual or
constructive — during the member's tenure; repeated material decision gates at which
intervention was practical.

The accretive machinery of §6.3 and §6.4 belongs to the second lane.

8.2 Severity

Seven factors, each 0/1/2, summing 0–14: decision centrality, stakes, evidentiary clarity,
notice before action, opportunity to cure, failure to cure, durability.

Bands: 0–4 routine · 5–7 serious · 8–11 major · 12–14 critical.

Durability is assessed against when reconsideration became impractical, not against
completion. Otherwise the test rewards the sequencing §6.1 condemns.

Severity factors and band placement are carried forward unchanged pending calibration,
so that existing fixtures remain interpretable.

8.3 One fact, one place

A single act may inform more than one dimension only when it answers genuinely different
questions. It may not be counted twice within a dimension, nor scored in a dimension and
then subtracted again as a free-standing adjustment.

9. Evidence standards

Every figure traces to a primary document. If a figure cannot be reproduced from the
document it is attributed to, it is left out and the omission stated.

Corpus figures go stale the moment transcripts are added. Re-derive; never copy.

Compare every per-member finding against the other eight before stating it. Claims that
sound damning are often the council norm.

Notice must precede the act it indicts. Check the chronology first.

Bound claims to what was examined. "Nothing in the record" implies exhaustive review;
"nothing in the transcripts, the agenda record, or the technical basis of the July 2026
item" is equally forceful and precisely true.

Gaps must be legible. Completeness is not a publishing gate, but "not yet examined"
must read differently from "examined and nothing found."

10. Calibration and safeguards

Weights, grade thresholds, participation coefficients, materiality weights and MGF
consequence mechanics are reserved to calibration.

The scoring scale is anchored to substantive standards, not to the observed
distribution. Grade thresholds shall be established only after blind calibration against
a representative fixture set, and shall not subsequently be adjusted to produce a desired
distribution.

Protocol: freeze a fixture set drawn from several members; two reviewers rate
independently and blind; resolve disagreement by refining definitions, never by
averaging unexplained judgments; measure agreement in order — attributability, dimension
applicability, participation role, materiality, rating, rationale.

If two reviewers cannot apply the rubric consistently, the rubric is not ready. That is a
finding, not a failure.

A fixture's inputs may not be adjusted after seeing its result.

10.1 Computational rules

  1. Spec controls code. Where they disagree, the implementation is wrong.
  2. Each derived quantity is computed exactly once. This rule exists because Civic Focus
    was derived independently in two files with a comment saying they must match and nothing
    enforcing it; it drifted twice, most recently by 0.30 on a pillar carrying 25% of the
    grade.
  3. Versioned scoring. Every published score identifies its spec and data version.
  4. Regression tests on frozen fixtures.
  5. No silent formula changes. Anything capable of moving a score requires a version bump
    and a changelog entry.
  6. Reproducible. A published score must be reconstructable from disclosed records and
    rules.
  7. No undocumented inputs. If a quantity can change a grade, it belongs in this document.

11. Relationship to the written scorecard

The number is constrained by this rubric. The prose may be more pointed — identifying
patterns, contradictions and consequences — provided it distinguishes documented fact,
framework judgment, inference, and opinion.

The model exists to make hard criticism more defensible, not less forceful.
