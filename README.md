# Council Scorecards

**Live site: <https://ericdf.github.io/council-scorecards/>**

An independent accountability record for Berkeley's nine councilmembers, built from the
primary record and checkable against it.

## What this repository is

Generated publication output. **Do not author here.** Every page is built from the
private source repository and overwritten on each publish — a change made here is
silently destroyed the next time the publisher runs.

- **Source:** `ericdf/Berkeley-council-scorecards` (private)
- **Build:** `build_publications.py`, driven by `publications.json`
- **Publish:** `./publish-scorecard.sh` from the source repo
- **Deploy:** GitHub Pages, serving this repository's root directory

Page assignment between this publication and the Berkeley Decision Project is by
editorial purpose, recorded explicitly in `publications.json`, not inferred from paths.

## What is measured, and why votes are not enough

The single most important fact about this council is that it votes together — the large
majority of recorded votes are unanimous, and the council votes as a block on most vote
events. Votes therefore cannot distinguish members.

What distinguishes them is *how they arrived at the shared vote*: what they asked first,
what they proposed instead, whether they moved a substitute, and whether they held a
stated position under pressure. Everything scored here is an attempt to observe that,
against the standard set out in the published methodology.

## Related publication

[Berkeley Decision Project](https://ericdf.github.io/berkeley-decision-project/) develops
frameworks, methods and worked examples for improving how Berkeley makes public decisions,
evaluable independently of any judgment about an individual officeholder.

The two projects share research, source material and a commitment to documented evidence,
but serve different purposes and are published separately. This one evaluates
officeholders; that one develops better approaches to public decisions.

## Gaps are legible on purpose

The record is open-ended, and waiting for completeness would mean publishing nothing. So
"not yet examined" is written to read differently from "examined and nothing found."
Silence is never presented as a verdict, and every scorecard carries a notice to that
effect.

## Corrections

Every figure traces to a primary document. Where a figure could not be reproduced from
the source it is attributed to, it has been dropped and the omission stated on the page.

Corrections are welcome and are published when received.
