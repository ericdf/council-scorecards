May 19 Budget Hearing — Revenue Measures Advance; Alternatives Discussed but Not Moved
Source: Berkeley City Council special and regular meetings, May 19, 2026 — annotated agendas and meeting captions (unofficial Zoom text; times are elapsed)
Adena Ishii Full Council · unscored
Correction

An earlier version of this record said that several members said "there is no way I could vote for a budget that closed Station 4" and then "voted unanimously for a proposed budget" on May 19. No vote on the budget was taken on May 19; the budget was adopted 9–0 on June 23, 2026. Only Councilmember Brent Blackaby made the Station 4 statement. The record also attributed it to Councilmember Mark Humbert, who did not make it, printed Councilmember Blackaby's name incorrectly, and said Councilmember Igor Tregub did not mention priority-based budgeting, which he did. The findings for Blackaby, Humbert and Tregub that rested on those statements have been withdrawn, along with their scores. The finding that no member moved or formally requested a structural alternative now appears as a Council-wide description below, because the record does not support differentiating members on that basis.

What Happened

At a special meeting on May 19, 2026, the Council voted 9–0 to affirm direction to staff to prepare ballot language for a $300 million general obligation bond and a half-cent sales tax (roll call ending 02:23:13). That evening's regular meeting held the first public hearing on the City Manager's proposed FY2027–28 budget. The Council heard 22 speakers, closed the hearing, and took no vote on the budget. The proposed budget uses one-time funds to keep Fire Station 4 open in FY2027 and says its closure "will be reconsidered for Fiscal Year 2028" if the sales tax fails.

During the hearing, Councilmember Blackaby said twice that he could not vote for a budget that closed Station 4 (04:46:05 and 06:28:32). The second time, he added that closure was "still hanging out as a future prospect." Councilmember O'Keefe said she had "really, really profound concerns about the prospect of closing Fire Station 4 if the tax measure does not pass" (06:26:07).

No member moved or formally requested a structural alternative to the revenue-or-cuts choice. Nobody moved to find offsetting cuts that would fund Station 4 from the baseline, to tie the bond or tax measure to a permanent general-fund floor for fire services, or to commission a review of the overtime-versus-staffing tradeoffs that drive the fire budget. Three members named alternatives without moving them. Mayor Ishii said she had asked staff to cost out mandatory time off for non-sworn employees if the sales tax fails (06:48:38). Councilmember Kesarwani asked "what can we do smarter?" and pointed to contract monitoring and competitive procurement (06:35:58). Councilmember Lunaparra listed reductions she would accept, including fewer council policy committees, consolidated commissions, lower council non-personnel budgets, and structurally reduced police overtime (06:40:45). Councilmembers Taplin (06:13:45) and Tregub (06:21:31) referred to priority-based budgeting after a public speaker raised it (06:01:27); neither proposed adopting it.
